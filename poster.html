The poster story
01What is shared across response demands?

Lexical access connects the sounds we hear to words stored in our mental lexicon.

Previous imaging studies compared words with pronounceable pseudowords. They found stronger responses to pseudowords in superior temporal regions, and to words in ventral temporal and inferior prefrontal regions.1, 2

But these differences also depend on attention and response demands. How much reflects shared lexical processing, and how much depends on what a task asks us to do?

Hold the spoken stimuli constant.
Change the response demands.

Compare the activity, the lexicality effects, and the information that transfers between tasks.

02The same sounds. Three tasks.

Twenty-two participants heard 80 spoken stimuli: 40 words and 40 pronounceable pseudowords.

Passive listening

Listen without making a behavioral response.

Lexical decision

Indicate by button press whether the stimulus is a word.

Overt repetition

Repeat the stimulus aloud.

Intracranial EEG lets us follow high-gamma activity (HGA) through time. We first identify task-related activation, then compare words and pseudowords within those activated networks. Finally, we test whether lexicality information generalizes from one task to another.

Recording and analysis

The poster reports 22 participants (10 female; mean age 32.4 years), 2,555 bipolar sEEG electrode pairs, and 1,596 pairs in cortical regions defined by the Destrieux atlas.3

HGA (70–200 Hz) was extracted around stimulus onset, offset and response onset and z-scored against the 0.5-second pre-cue baseline. The interactive views below provide the available stimulus-onset and stimulus-offset summaries.

03HGA activation

Shared auditory activation, followed by task-dependent recruitment.

Significant HGA increases relative to baseline reveal auditory temporal recruitment across all three tasks. Active tasks recruit additional frontotemporal responses later in time.

Where activity rises is the first step. Next, ask where that activity differs between words and pseudowords. Explore HGA contrast ↓

04HGA contrast

Shared and task-dependent lexicality effects.

Within the task-activated networks, we compare HGA between words and pseudowords and identify significant lexicality effects in either direction.

These contrasts suggest two levels of lexical processing: one present without a required response, and another recruited by response demands but shared across output modalities.

05Shared lexical representations across tasks

Does a word–pseudoword distinction learned in one task carry over to another?

To test for shared lexical representations, we trained a linear classifier on HGA to distinguish words from pseudowords in one task, then tested it on the other tasks.

The findings support two levels of shared lexical representations:

STG / STS

Representations are present even when no behavioral response is required.

Ventral temporal and prefrontal regions

Representations are recruited by response demands but shared across button-press and spoken responses.

06A shared foundation. Additional recruitment.
Lexical processing includes a component present without behavioral output and additional components recruited by output demands yet shared across output modalities.
