THE FULL STORY
01THE QUESTIONWhat does recognizing a word require?

We recognize spoken words even when we do not need to respond. But speaking or making a decision may recruit additional processing.

Lexical access links speech sounds to stored word knowledge. Comparing words with pronounceable pseudowords helps us ask where, when and under which task demands that processing occurs.

Functional imaging reports stronger pseudoword responses in superior temporal gyrus, and stronger word responses in ventral temporal and anterior inferior frontal regions. How these patterns unfold in time and depend on behavioral output remains the central question. [1]

How to read this prototype

Task colors: Passive is green, Decision is burgundy, Repetition is blue. Activation uses a warm magnitude scale. Contrast is Nonword − Word: warm indicates Nonword > Word; cool indicates Word > Nonword.

Contours enclose each synthetic field above a fixed display threshold. Hatching is their geometric overlap at the same time and in the same contrast direction. It is not a significance test or evidence for shared coding. The regional traces use task colors and illustrative units.

These task distributions suggest output-related recruitment. Detecting an effect in active tasks but not passive listening does not by itself establish a task × lexicality interaction.
